Part A
Automatic Voice 360 Pages

2. What is a Voice 360 Page?

A Voice 360 Page is the official public profile of a verified business, professional, shop or eligible service provider. It is different from a user profile and different from a Community Group.

3. Pages created automatically

Voice 360 will automatically create a dedicated page for:

  • Every verified business in the Business Directory
  • Every verified professional in the Professionals Directory
  • Every approved shop in the Marketplace
  • Every verified travel business offering travel packages
  • Every business publishing Deals and Offers, provided the business already exists in the Business Directory

Users must not create these pages manually. The page is created automatically when the related business, professional or shop is approved.

4. Page eligibility

Automatic pages are available only for verified businesses, verified professionals, approved Marketplace shops, verified travel providers and eligible Deals and Offers businesses. Unverified or rejected records should not receive a public page.

5. Avoiding duplicate pages

The same business must not receive multiple separate pages simply because it appears in multiple Voice 360 modules. A business may exist in the Business Directory, the Marketplace, Deals and Offers and Travel 360 at the same time. If those records belong to the same verified business, they must connect to one main Voice 360 Page.

That page can show different sections or tabs — About, Posts, Products, Services, Offers, Travel Packages, Reviews. The backend should use a single verified business identity or organisation ID to prevent duplicate pages.

6. Page ownership

Each page has one primary owner, optional additional managers, optional editors or staff, and followers. The page owner is linked to the verified business or professional record and may assign approved staff permissions later.

Owner
Full page control · edit page information · publish content · manage staff · respond to reviews · view analytics
Manager
Publish and manage content · respond to comments and messages · manage offers and services
Editor
Create and edit posts · upload photos and videos
Follower
View follower-only content · comment according to platform rules · review and rate where eligible · interact with page content

7. Page permissions

Owner and authorised managers
Create posts · edit page information · upload photos and videos · publish products, services, packages or offers · comment on posts · reply to users · manage page content · respond to reviews
Followers
View the page and its content · comment according to page rules · react to content · leave reviews and ratings where eligible · share page content · receive page updates
Non-followers
View public page information and content · follow the page · share public content. They cannot post on the page or comment unless platform rules allow it after following.

Maintain separate permission sets for page owner, page staff, follower and non-follower visitor.

Part C & D
Activity Zone and its access rules

11. What is Activity Zone?

The Activity Zone is the administrative area a user selects in their Voice 360 profile. It determines where the user is allowed to participate as a local community member: creating posts, commenting, reacting as an eligible local member, reporting local issues, creating or voting in polls, joining local discussions, participating in local events and supporting member-only petitions.

The Activity Zone does not prevent the user from viewing or following other communities.

12. Requirements before community participation

  • Complete the required profile information
  • Verify the Voice 360 account
  • Complete the required location fields
  • Select an Activity Zone
  • Pass any required location validation

Until these steps are complete the user may view public community content but should not receive full participation permission.

Suggested status message
“Complete your profile, verify your account and select your Activity Zone to participate in local communities.”

13. Two access directions

Rule A
Higher-level Activity Zone

When a user selects a higher administrative level, they can participate in that area and in the administrative units located under it.

Rule B
Lower-level Activity Zone

When a user selects a lower administrative level, they can participate only in that exact unit and its direct parent units. They cannot participate in sibling or parallel areas.

14. Example — District selected

If a user selects a District as their Activity Zone, they can participate in the selected District Community and in every Upazila, applicable Thana, City Corporation, Pourashava, Union, City Ward, Pourashava Ward and Union Ward within that District.

If the selected Activity Zone is Natore District, the user may participate in official communities geographically contained within Natore District. The backend must use the location hierarchy, not text name matching.

15. Example — Union Ward selected

May participate in

Its parent Union
Its parent Upazila
Its applicable parent Thana, where the hierarchy uses one

16. Strict parent-only hierarchy rule

For a lower-level Activity Zone, the access path must always follow the selected area’s direct ancestry.

Union Ward 3 → parent Union → parent Upazila or Thana → parent District → parent Division, if enabled

The system must not calculate access from the fact that two areas share a District. It must follow the actual parent chain: selected unit, direct parent, parent of that parent, continuing upward to the highest enabled level. Sibling wards, neighbouring unions, other upazilas, parallel city areas and any unit outside the selected unit’s direct hierarchy are incorrect access.

17. Important access interpretation

Geographical containment

A District contains many lower-level units. District selection gives broad participation access to its descendants.

Direct ancestry

A Ward belongs to one specific parent chain. Ward selection gives narrow access to that Ward and its direct parents.

A user can never move sideways across sibling locations. This is the core Activity Zone rule.

Part E & F
Viewing, following and community roles

18. Viewing other communities

Users may search and view any public Community Group: search any administrative community, open public community pages, view public posts and announcements, follow the community, share public content and receive selected public updates. Viewing access does not make the user a participating local member.

19. Participation outside Activity Zone

When a community is outside the user’s authorised Activity Zone hierarchy, the user cannot create posts, comment as a local community member, react where reactions require local membership, vote in member-only polls, create local issues, participate in member-only discussions, sign member-only petitions, RSVP to restricted local events or access private member information.

The UI must clearly show
“You are viewing this community as a visitor.”
“Follow this community to receive public updates. Participation is limited to verified members of this Activity Zone.”
Do not show a normal Join button.

20. Follow and participation are different

Follow relationship
Receive public updates · save the community · view its public content
Activity Zone participation permission
Post · comment · react as an eligible member · vote · report issues · participate in member-only activities

Following a community must never automatically give participation permission.

25

Part H & I
Backend permission calculation and zone changes

30. Required backend process

When a user selects or changes their Activity Zone, the backend should:

  1. Verify the selected area ID.
  2. Confirm the user’s profile and account verification status.
  3. Identify the area’s type.
  4. Load its full parent chain.
  5. Load descendants only when the selected Activity Zone level allows descendant access.
  6. Generate the authorised community ID list.
  7. Save the access calculation.
  8. Remove permissions that no longer apply.
  9. Add new permissions.
  10. Record the change in an audit log.
  11. Update relevant community counts.
  12. Notify the user.

31. Access is calculated, not manually assigned

The system should not create thousands of separate access rules per user. Access is calculated from the user’s Activity Zone ID, the selected area type, the parent-child hierarchy, verification status, community status and access policy. The backend may cache the result for performance, but the hierarchy database remains the source of truth.

32. Suggested access result

Returned fields
Community ID · community name · community type · access relationship · participation allowed · follow status · visitor status · reason for permission
Possible access relationships
Selected Activity Zone · parent of Activity Zone · descendant of selected higher-level zone · outside Activity Zone · followed only

33. Changing Activity Zone

Users should not be able to change their Activity Zone repeatedly without control. The process may include confirmation, address verification, a supporting document where necessary, a waiting period and admin review for suspicious changes.

When the Activity Zone changes:

  1. Recalculate community participation rights.
  2. Remove permissions from communities that no longer qualify.
  3. Keep followed communities as followed, where appropriate.
  4. Add permissions for the new hierarchy.
  5. Keep the user’s previous posts visible.
  6. Mark old posts with the location status applicable when they were posted.
  7. Store the complete change history.
Part J, K & L
Parliamentary communities, navigation and labels

34. Parliamentary Community remains separate

The Administrative Community system stays separate from the Parliamentary Zone. Voice 360 will have administrative Community Groups based on local administrative units, and a fixed set of 300 Parliamentary Communities.

Left Menu → Community
Administrative communities
Left Menu → Parliamentary Zone
The user’s parliamentary community

35. Parliamentary membership

The user’s Parliamentary Community is calculated from the parliamentary constituency linked to their verified profile location. A user normally belongs to one Parliamentary Community, and it must not be selected through the normal Activity Zone hierarchy alone.

The central location database maps the user’s address or selected location to the correct constituency. No manual Join, Pending Approval or Leave Community flow is required.

Part M
Final development rules
  1. Users cannot create official geographical community groups.
  2. Each administrative unit has only one official group.
  3. Pages are generated automatically for eligible verified records.
  4. The same business should have one page across multiple modules.
  5. Users must complete and verify their profile before community participation.
  6. Activity Zone determines participation rights.
  7. Following a community does not grant participation rights.
  8. A higher-level zone may grant access to its authorised descendants.
  9. A lower-level zone grants access only to itself and its direct parent path.
  10. Users cannot participate in sibling or parallel areas.
  11. Users may view and follow other public communities.
  12. Use one central address and location system across all modules.
  13. Use unique area IDs, not area names, for relationships.
  14. Keep Parliamentary Communities separate from Administrative Communities.
  15. Reuse existing Voice 360 posts, comments, reactions, messages, notifications and reporting systems.
  16. All permission checks must be enforced by the backend, not only hidden in the UI.
  17. Store Activity Zone and location change history.
  18. Prevent duplicate pages and duplicate official communities.
  19. Support dynamic urban, rural and municipal address paths.
  20. Every location-based record should link to the central location database.

Why this structure matters

Facebook Groups are created and controlled manually. Voice 360 communities are officially structured, geographically accurate, automatically generated, connected to verified users, based on real administrative boundaries and protected from duplicate or fake local groups.

The Activity Zone concept balances openness and local control: anyone can discover and follow communities, only eligible local users can participate, lower-level users cannot interfere in neighbouring communities, and district-level users can participate more broadly when that is intentionally allowed.

The unified address system is equally essential. Without it, Business Directory, Professionals, Marketplace, Community, Jobs, Property and other modules could all use different location data and produce inconsistent results.

One policy to confirm before backend development

The current rule lets a user who selects District as their Activity Zone participate in every lower community within that District — very broad posting rights. That can work as designed, but the system must clearly distinguish three things: the user’s verified residential or primary location, the user’s selected Activity Zone, and the communities where they hold participation rights. Otherwise many users will select District only to unlock everything beneath it.

Target structure: the verified address determines primary local identity, while the Activity Zone determines the approved operating area under platform eligibility rules.

District-level access could later require stronger verification, an organisation role, a professional role or admin approval, with ordinary residents using their actual local unit and its parent hierarchy. This may not ship at launch, but the regulations should be introduced gradually — the safeguard preserves the strict local identity the concept is built around.
