How to read this document
Sections 1–4 are the architecture: rules, site map, routes and screens. Sections 5–8 are the contract: data model, ownership, permissions and state machines. Sections 9–12 are the build: corner cases, notifications, API surface and sequencing. Section 13 is the decision register — every item there blocks something, and each carries the options so you can decide in the room rather than wait.
Anything marked OPEN is a decision we have not been given. It is stated with the options and the consequence of each, never guessed silently. Anything marked ASSUMED is our inference from the designs — safe to build, but push back if it contradicts a business rule you hold.

1 · The rules that govern everything

Nine invariants. Every screen, endpoint and permission in this document derives from them. If a requirement contradicts one of these, the requirement is wrong or the rule has changed — escalate rather than special-case.
R1 · Membership is automatic
Verified profile + constituency = membership. There is no join request, no approval queue and no pending state anywhere in this module. Source: CEO revised scope, "Membership is automatic · 300 communities".
R2 · One seat per citizen, 300 seats total
One community per parliamentary seat, titled by its area. A citizen is a member of exactly one at a time. Membership is derived state, never a row a user creates.
R3 · Viewing is open, participation is scoped
Any citizen may view and follow any of the 300. Posting, commenting, voting in polls, reporting issues and pledging are available only in their own seat. The other 299 render the same screen in visitor state (PC-03.V).
R4 · One template, 300 instances
PC-03 is a single build parameterised by seat. Nothing is authored per constituency except its content. No per-seat forks in code or design.
R5 · Two doors, one room
Voter Directory, Development & Ideas and Elections & Representatives are reachable both as Area Overview cards and as Community Home tabs. These are one page rendered in two entry contexts — never two implementations. Frontend: one route, one component, entry recorded for analytics only.
R6 · AI is advisory and grounded, never authoritative
Every AI answer carries its source manifest with freshness stamps. Ranked ordering comes from the platform's own priority score, not model opinion. Place names are lifted from source records, never generated. Where an area has no published data the assistant says so (PC-07.5) — it never substitutes national or neighbouring figures.
R7 · Violet is AI-only
#5b3fd1 and its tints are reserved for AI surfaces across the whole product. Non-AI features that previously used violet were moved to teal #17838a or blue #33569b. Do not reintroduce violet outside PC-07.x and PC-04.4.
R8 · The app never casts a vote
PC-05.4 "I will vote" is a public pledge. Voice 360 informs and reminds; the ballot happens at the polling centre. No endpoint, label or notification may imply otherwise. Community polls (PC-03.6V) are a separate, non-electoral mechanism.
R9 · Nothing is published without an accountable author
Announcements carry an official role. Issues carry a citizen. AI drafts are always reviewed by a human before submission (PC-07.4 → PC-04.3). The assistant cannot create an issue on its own.

5 · Data model

Entities implied by the 32 screens. Field lists are the minimum the designs require, not an exhaustive schema. Derived entities must never be user-writable.
Seat EC · reference data
id · code (Dhaka-10) · name · slug · division · district · wards[] · population · registeredVoters · verifiedVoters · boundaryVersion
Exactly 300 rows. Read-only from the EC import. boundaryVersion exists so delimitation can be handled without rewriting history — see C-4.
Citizen EC + USER
id · nid · name · phone · email · address · verificationState · seatId · wardId · roles[] · verifiedAt
seatId is set at verification. It is the single input to every permission decision in this module.
Membership CALC · derived
citizenId · seatId · since · state
Derived from Citizen.verificationState == verified && Citizen.seatId (R1). No create or join endpoint may exist. Materialise it if you need the index, but never expose a writer.
Follow USER
citizenId · seatId · createdAt
The only relationship a visitor may create. Following is explicitly not membership and grants no write permission.
Issue USER
id · seatId · authorId · category · wardId · title · description · attachments[] · existingSince · state · priorityScore · clusterId · votes · createdAt
clusterId groups duplicate reports so twenty drainage complaints read as one problem. priorityScore is the single ordering authority — PC-04.1 and the AI answers must both read it (R6).
Poll · PollVote ADMIN + USER
Poll: id · seatId · authorId · question · options[] · state · opensAt · closesAt
PollVote: pollId · citizenId · optionId · castAt
Unique constraint on (pollId, citizenId). Seat membership must be re-checked server-side at cast time, not trusted from the client.
Announcement · Post · Event · RSVP USER / ADMIN
Announcement: id · seatId · authorId · authorRole · title · body · publishedAt
Event: id · seatId · title · startsAt · venue · createdBy · state · RSVP: eventId · citizenId · state
authorRole is stored on the announcement, not looked up at read time — a role change must not rewrite history (R9, C-8).
Representative · Candidate · VotePledge ADMIN + EC
Representative: id · seatId · office · name · party · termStart · termEnd · record[]
Candidate: id · seatId · electionId · name · party · manifesto · nominationState
VotePledge: electionId · citizenId · pledgedAt
Candidates must carry identical field sets — the design forbids promoted placement. VotePledge is a pledge record only (R8).
AIConversation · AIMessage · SourceManifest EXT + CALC
AIConversation: id · citizenId · seatId · startedAt · archivedAt
AIMessage: id · conversationId · role · body · elapsedMs · sourceManifestId
SourceManifest: id · entries[{dataset, recordCount, lastSyncAt}]
Every assistant message must reference a manifest, including no-data answers — that is what makes an answer checkable (R6). Conversations are keyed to seat: moving seat archives the old history rather than migrating it.

8 · State machines

Five machines. Transitions not listed here do not exist — reject them rather than tolerating them.
Membership
unverified → verified_no_seat → member(seatId)
member(A) → member(B)  // address change, see C-3
No pending or requested state exists (R1). verified_no_seat occurs when verification passes but the address does not resolve to a seat — see C-2.
Issue
draft → submitted → triaged → in_progress → resolved
submitted → rejected  // with reason, always visible to author
triaged → merged(clusterId)  // duplicate handling
Drafts are device-local until submitted. Merged issues keep their own record and redirect to the cluster — the author must never lose their submission.
Poll
draft → open → closed → published
open → cancelled  // votes discarded, stated publicly
Results are hidden from a citizen until they have voted, and from everyone until closed. Publishing posts the outcome back to the Feed.
Election
scheduled → nomination_open → nomination_closed → campaigning
    → polling_day → ended → result_published
Drives PC-05.2 (candidate list visibility), PC-05.3 (countdown) and PC-05.4 (pledge availability). The pledge closes at polling_day. Results are EC-sourced, never computed by us.
AI answer
idle → thinking → answered(manifest)
thinking → no_data(manifest with gap)  // PC-07.5
thinking → error  // model or network failure, distinct from no_data
no_data is not an error. It is raised by a data-presence check before answering, not by model uncertainty. Conflating the two breaks R6 and is the single most important distinction in the AI build.

9 · Corner cases

The cases that will otherwise be discovered in production. Each carries the required behaviour; where behaviour is not yet decided it is marked OPEN and cross-referenced to section 13.
# Case Required behaviour
C-1Citizen is unverifiedParliamentary Zone is viewable but every write is blocked. Show the verification route, not a generic permission error.
C-2Verified, but address does not resolve to a seatState verified_no_seat. Citizen sees all 300 as a visitor and is prompted to correct their address. Never silently assign a default seat.
C-3Citizen moves to another seatMembership moves. Existing posts, issues and votes stay attributed to the seat they were made in, with the status that applied then. AI history is archived, not migrated.
C-4Seat boundaries are redrawn (delimitation)OPEN — D-6. boundaryVersion exists so history can be preserved, but whether communities merge, split or archive is a business decision.
C-5Member votes in a poll, then loses membershipThe vote stands — it was valid when cast. Do not recount retroactively. Do not allow a second vote in the new seat's copy of a different poll to be linked.
C-6Two accounts claim the same NIDVerification must reject the second. NID uniqueness is the identity guarantee the whole permission model rests on.
C-7Representative dies, resigns, or loses a by-electionClose the term with termEnd; do not delete. PC-05.1 shows current office-holders; past holders remain in the record.
C-8Announcement author loses their role afterwardsThe announcement keeps the authorRole stored at publish time. Never resolve roles at read time (R9).
C-9Event is cancelled after RSVPsNotify every RSVP holder. Keep the event visible with a cancelled state rather than removing it — attendees may already have travelled.
C-10Issue created while offlineDraft persists on device with its attachments. Submit on reconnect. Never lose typed content to a connection drop.
C-11Attachment upload fails mid-wayShow per-file progress and a cancel control. A failed file must not block submission of the rest.
C-12Session expires mid-flowPreserve the draft, re-authenticate, return to the last safe step. Applies to issue creation and the AI follow-up wizard.
C-13AI asked about a different constituencyAnswer only for the citizen's own seat, and say which seat the answer covers. Do not silently answer about another area.
C-14AI asked where the seat has no published dataPC-07.5. Name the missing dataset, offer to raise it as an issue. Never substitute national or neighbouring figures (R6).
C-15Model call times out or errorsDistinct from no-data. Show elapsed time honestly, offer retry. The wizard draft must survive.
C-16Two citizens report the same problemCluster them. Both authors keep their record; the cluster carries the count shown in PC-04.1 and quoted by the AI.
C-17Invitee's address maps to a different seatThey join their seat, not the inviter's. Stated on PC-06 so the inviter is not surprised.
C-18Government dataset sync is stale or failsShow the last successful sync date, not the publication date, and never hide staleness. AI answers must carry the same stamp.
C-19Poll closes while a citizen is votingReject at cast time with a clear message and show results. Server time is authoritative, not the client's.
C-20Content is reported by a memberQueue for moderation. Requires the admin console — until then reports accumulate with no reviewer (see section 12).

12 · Build sequencing

What blocks what. The admin console is the largest single dependency in the module — six screens render content that nothing can currently create.
Phase 1 — buildable today
PC-01, PC-01.L, PC-02, PC-03 + tabs .1 .2 .8 .9, PC-04.1–.3, PC-05.1–.4, PC-06 and its two states, PC-03.V. Everything here depends only on the EC import, citizen input and computed values.
Blocked on the admin console
PC-03.3 Announcements, PC-03.6/.6C/.6V/.6R Polls, PC-03.7C Create Event, plus issue triage behind PC-04.2 and the moderation queue behind C-20. The reader screens can ship, but they will be empty and reports will accumulate unreviewed.
Blocked on the AI platform
PC-07.1–.5 and PC-04.4. Needs the model integration, the source manifest service, the data-presence check that produces no_data, and on-device speech-to-text. The manifest service is the part most often underestimated — without it R6 cannot hold.
Designed elsewhere — connect, do not design
Petitions (5 screens), Become a Candidate (13), Apply for Nomination (10), Support Candidates (1), Events 360 (18), Local Area Election (17). These have their own flow documents. Parliamentary routes into them; it does not reimplement them.
Existing platform modules — connect only
Famous places, local directory (Education · Health · Markets), Latest in your area, local groups, popular topics, community stats, share sheet, messaging, calling, profile. No new design work.

13 · Decision register

Every open decision, with options and consequences. Nothing here is guessed elsewhere in this document.
D-1 · Demo constituency
Source screens disagreed — Area Overview said Dhaka-10 (Mirpur), others said Dhaka-15. Standardised on Dhaka-10 (Mirpur) throughout. Confirm, or tell us to switch.
D-2 · Who publishes announcements
Option A — MP-side composer. The MP posts directly from their phone. Fastest and most personal; needs a role-gated composer and no review step.
Option B — admin console only. Everything routes through the back office for review. Slower but moderated; needs no MP-side build.
These are different products, and the choice changes both scope and the trust model.
D-3 · Constituency selection at verification
CEO documents say membership follows "verified profile + constituency". Whether the seat is derived silently from the address or explicitly picked by the citizen is not stated. This changes the verification flow and case C-2.
D-4 · Petitions routing and signing rights
Five screens are designed. Open: how PC-03's petitions tab routes into them, and whether signing is gated on seat membership (consistent with polls) or opened more widely.
D-5 · Events tab vs Events 360
PC-03.7 is a simple seat-filtered list. Events 360 is a full 18-screen module with AI search, digital passes and audience targeting. Recommendation: PC-03.7 routes into Events 360 pre-filtered to the seat. Confirm before either team builds an event list.
D-6 · Delimitation policy
When seat boundaries are redrawn, do communities merge, split, or archive and restart? Affects C-4, historical attribution and the 300-community invariant. Not urgent to build, urgent to decide before the schema is frozen.
D-7 · AI assumptions to confirm
Stated in the definitions as our inference: five-attachment cap on issue drafts, 30-day rolling windows for reports and active citizens, verified publishers only for the news source, 30-second model timeout, on-device speech-to-text with audio never uploaded. All are safe defaults; all are ours, not yours.
D-8 · Moderation SLA
Reported content (C-20) and issue triage both queue for a reviewer. No response time is defined. Without one, the reporting routes in the UI promise something the platform cannot keep.
