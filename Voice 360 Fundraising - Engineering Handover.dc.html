How to read this document
Sections 1–4 are the architecture: rules, site map, routes and screens. Sections 5–8 are the contract: data model, ownership, permissions and state machines. Sections 9–12 are the build: corner cases, notifications, API surface and sequencing. Section 13 is the decision register — every item there blocks something, and each carries the options so you can decide in the room rather than wait.
Anything marked OPEN is a decision we have not been given. It is stated with the options and the consequence of each, never guessed silently. Anything marked ASSUMED is our inference from the designs — safe to build, but push back if it contradicts a business rule you hold.
This module moves real money and publishes public ledgers. Two rules override every other consideration in this document: no client ever computes a balance, and nothing a donor sees about where money went is written by anyone other than the server.

1 · The rules that govern everything

Ten invariants. Every screen, endpoint and permission in this document derives from them. If a requirement contradicts one of these, the rule wins until someone changes it here.
#RuleWhat it forces
R1The server owns every numberRaised totals, progress percentages, donor counts, days left, statement totals and available-to-withdraw are read-only fields on the response. The client never sums donations it holds.
R2A donation exists before a payment is attemptedFR-07.4 Review creates the donation in pending and returns an id. The PSP is then called against that id, so every attempt — including the ones that fail at FR-07.6 — is reconcilable.
R3Success is declared by the webhook, not the screenFR-07.5 Processing polls; FR-07.7 renders only after the server marks the donation captured. A returning app that never saw FR-07.7 must still find the donation captured.
R4One payment component, both flowsFR-07.3 – FR-07.7 and FR-09.3 – FR-09.5 are the same component with a different intent object (FR-07.6O states this explicitly). Do not build two payment stacks.
R5Every capture emits a receiptA captured donation or sponsorship charge writes an immutable receipt (FR-10.2) and a row in the annual statement (FR-10.3). Receipts are never edited — corrections are new rows.
R6The transparency report is a projection, not an entry formFR-05 and FR-05.D render only server-side ledger entries tied to a payout or a captured donation. No screen in this module writes to it.
R7Nothing publishes without verificationA campaign request (FR-08) is invisible to donors until a verifier approves it. Beneficiaries in FR-03/FR-09.0 are onboarded by admin only; no citizen creates a beneficiary.
R8Organisers never touch money directlyFR-13.4 requests a withdrawal against a verified account; FR-13.5 is a request state. Release is a back-office action outside this module.
R9Recurring giving is a subscription with its own lifeFR-07.1M and every FR-09 plan create a mandate. Each cycle is its own charge with its own receipt and its own failure path (FR-09.5F); a failed cycle never cancels the sponsorship silently.
R10Identity is optional to the public, never to the ledgerA donor may hide their name from FR-06.D Donors and from the organiser's list. The receipt, statement and internal record always carry the real identity.

6 · Source of truth

Every element in the Fundraising definitions document carries one of these tags. The tag decides who may write the value — and in this module, who is liable for it.
CALCServer-derived. Raised, progress, donor count, days left, available to withdraw, statement totals, next charge date. Read-only everywhere.
ADMINWritten in the back office: beneficiary records and plans, verification decisions, moderated updates, ledger notes, payout release.
ORGWritten by the campaign organiser: story, cover, updates, thank-you messages, withdrawal requests.
USERWritten by the citizen: amount, anonymity, message, contact details, request drafts, plan choice.
PSPOwned by the payment provider: attempt status, failure codes, provider reference, capture time. The app records, never edits.
EXTExternal or static: cause taxonomy, fee schedule, legal copy, trade/NGO registry checks used during verification.

9 · Corner cases

The cases that will otherwise be discovered in production — and in a money module, discovered by a donor. Each carries the required behaviour; where behaviour is a business call it is marked and repeated in §13.
#CaseRequired behaviour
C-01App is killed on FR-07.5 ProcessingOn next launch, resolve the intent server-side. If captured, show the receipt; if still unknown, show a "we are confirming" state — never a second charge.
C-02Donor taps Confirm twiceIdempotency key on FR-07.4 returns the same donation id. One attempt, one charge.
C-03Webhook arrives before the client leaves FR-07.5Poll picks it up and advances to FR-07.7. Server state is authoritative in both directions.
C-04Webhook never arrivesReconciliation job queries the PSP for open attempts. FR-07.5T is a waiting state, not a verdict.
C-05Campaign hits its goal mid-checkoutAllow the donation to complete; overfunding is permitted and shown as over 100%. OPEN — whether a hard cap exists.
C-06Campaign is suspended mid-checkoutReject at FR-07.4 with a clear reason. If already captured, refund automatically and notify.
C-07Beneficiary reaches sponsor capacity mid-flowFR-09.0A. Offer the waiting list or a similar beneficiary; never take a mandate that cannot be honoured.
C-08Beneficiary leaves the programme with active sponsorsStop future charges, mark completed, notify each sponsor with a reassignment offer. OPEN — reassign or refund the current cycle.
C-09Monthly charge fails on a saved walletFR-09.5F. Retry on a defined schedule, notify each time, then past_due. The sponsorship stays visible in FR-11.1.
C-10Sponsor changes the amount mid-cycleFR-11.4 amends the mandate from the next charge. The current cycle is untouched and the screen says so.
C-11Donor asks to be removed from the public donor list after givingAnonymity is editable after the fact on FR-10.2; the ledger keeps identity (R10).
C-12Organiser requests more than the available balanceServer rejects with the available figure. Never rely on the FR-13.4 field being right.
C-13Two withdrawal requests in flightOne open payout per campaign. The second is refused with the first's reference.
C-14Payout fails at the bank after releaseReturn the amount to available, write a ledger adjustment visible in FR-05, notify the organiser.
C-15Refund issued after the annual statement was generatedRegenerate the statement and mark it revised; do not silently change a downloaded total.
C-16Duplicate campaign request from the same citizenFR-08.4D. Dedupe server-side on citizen + type + window and link to the existing reference.
C-17Uploaded document is unreadable or fails a scanReject at upload with the reason. A request never reaches review with an unscanned file.
C-18Request approved while the citizen's draft is openDraft becomes read-only and routes to FR-12.2. No lost-update conflict.
C-19Organiser loses the role while managing the campaignFR-13.x returns 403 on the next call and the app falls back to FR-06. Never cache the permission.
C-20Donor message contains abuse or personal dataQueue for moderation before it appears on FR-13.3 or reaches a beneficiary. Hold, do not publish then remove.
C-21Fees change between intent creation and captureFreeze the fee on the intent. The donor pays what FR-07.4 showed.
C-22Ledger and campaign total disagreeThe ledger wins. Surface a reconciliation alert internally; FR-05 must never contradict FR-06.
C-23Citizen deletes their account with an active sponsorshipCancel future cycles, retain receipts and ledger rows for the statutory period. OPEN — retention window.

12 · Build sequencing

The payment component and the ledger are the two dependencies that block everything else. Build them once, correctly, before any surface that reads a number.
Wave 1 · foundation ledger + money types + fee schedule + idempotency PSP integration, webhook, reconciliation job shared payment component (R4) blocks everything below Wave 2 · read paths FR-01 · FR-02 (+ .S .F .L .N) · FR-04 FR-06 (+ .U .D .C) FR-05 · FR-05.D needs the ledger from Wave 1 Wave 3 · one-off giving FR-07.1 – FR-07.7 with every failure state receipts (FR-10.2), FR-10.1 · FR-10.4 Wave 4 · recurring beneficiaries FR-03 · FR-09.0 (+ .0A) mandates FR-09.1 – FR-09.5 (+ .5F .5C) FR-11.1 – FR-11.4 · dunning schedule Wave 5 · supply side FR-08.1 – FR-08.5 (+ .1E .1D .4D .5C) FR-12.1 · FR-12.2 verifier console (outside this module) blocks approval end to end Wave 6 · organiser FR-13.1 – FR-13.5 · payout accounts · finance release FR-10.3 annual statement generation

13 · Decision register

Every open decision, with options and consequences. Nothing here is guessed elsewhere in this document.
#DecisionOptions and consequence
D-01Who bears the payment feeDonor pays on top (raised = pledge) or the campaign absorbs it (raised = net). Changes FR-07.4, every receipt and FR-05. Blocks Wave 1.
D-02Platform fee, if anyNone, fixed percentage, or opt-in tip. A tip needs a control on FR-07.1 that does not exist yet.
D-03Payment providers at launchbKash / Nagad / card / bank. Each adds a method row on FR-07.3 and its own failure codes for FR-07.6.
D-04Are recurring mandates supported by the chosen providerIf not, FR-07.1M and all of Journey C become reminder-driven manual charges — a different product. Blocks Wave 4.
D-05Minimum and maximum donationDrives FR-07.1E copy and KYC thresholds. A high ceiling may require source-of-funds checks.
D-06Dunning schedule and lapse windowHow many retries over how many days before lapsed, and whether the beneficiary's slot is released. C-09, FR-09.5F.
D-07Overfunding capUncapped, hard stop at 100%, or organiser-set ceiling. C-05.
D-08Refund policy and windowWhether donors can request a refund at all, and who approves. Determines whether FR-10.2 needs an action.
D-09Withdrawal rulesMinimum amount, frequency, milestone-based release, proof-of-spend requirement. Shapes FR-13.4 and the payout machine.
D-10Verification standard for a campaign requestWhich documents, which registry checks, what SLA. FR-08.5's stated review window depends on it.
D-11Beneficiary consent and data retentionGuardian consent for minors, how long stories and photographs stay published, and what a sponsor may see. §7 privacy box, C-23.
D-12Tax-deductible receiptsIf claimed, receipts and FR-10.3 need a registration number and a legally fixed format. Retro-fitting this is expensive.
D-13Transparency granularityPer-transaction (as designed) or aggregated. Per-transaction is the stronger promise and the harder operational commitment.
D-14Public campaign linksShareable slugs require a signed-out read-only FR-06. §3.
